

Over summer break I bought high-speed rail tickets to take Celine back to our hometown in Hubei. We chose to ride the metro to Shenzhen North Station.

Before we go in, I go over the safety rules: Stay close when it's crowded. Hold hands or hold the rail on escalators. Stand behind the yellow line on the platform.

We enter at Exit A of Baolong Station and take the escalator down. After we're inside, we put our bags through security first. If we're carrying liquids, they need a separate liquid check. Then we follow the signs to the gates and the platform. If we're not sure, we ask at customer service—we don't guess.

When Celine was in kindergarten, she didn't need her own card. I'd swipe first, and she'd follow right behind—no stopping at the gate. Now she uses her child transit card, goes through the concession lane, and rides at half price.

On the platform I repeat three short lines: "Stand behind the line." "Let people off first, then get on." "Mind the gap when you step in." Short lines work best in a noisy place.

Once we're on board, we move away from the doors and hold a pole. If there are no seats, we stand steady and keep our bags tucked in. If we get a seat, she sits properly and we get ready to get off before our stop.

We watch the display for the next stop and listen to the train announcements.

For example: "Next stop, Buji. Please exit in the direction shown above this door. You may transfer to Line 5 here, or continue to Shenzhen North Station for the railway."

When we transfer, we read the arrows and line numbers and walk with the crowd without pushing. At our stop we confirm the right exit and keep a steady pace in the corridors—no panic, no running.

For us, the metro isn't just transportation. It's a daily practice ground for public safety, polite behavior, and practical English.
