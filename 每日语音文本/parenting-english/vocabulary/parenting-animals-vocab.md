

I use animal vocabulary with Celine in three scenes: books, parks, and guessing games. At home, we start with familiar animals like cat, dog, rabbit, and duck. Then I add features: "The rabbit has long ears." "The duck has webbed feet." "The giraffe has a long neck."

When we go outside, we connect words to real observation. If we see birds near water, I ask, "Is it a duck or a goose?" She points, describes color and movement, and gives her guess.

At night, we play a riddle game. I describe one animal without saying the name: "It is big, gray, and has a trunk." She answers, "Elephant!" Then we switch roles and she gives clues.

This routine teaches more than nouns. It builds sentence patterns, listening attention, and confidence in speaking. Animal words are easy for kids to love, so they are perfect for daily English momentum.
