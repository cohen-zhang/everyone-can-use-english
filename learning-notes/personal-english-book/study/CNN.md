### 【C】核心专题：为什么全世界又挤回月球（01:50–05:40）★精听主攻段

> WIRE: Now, at the same time the U.S. is accomplishing this feat, other countries are looking to make their giant leaps to the moon as well. Our space and defense correspondent Kristin Fisher has more.

> KRISTIN FISHER, CNN SPACE AND DEFENSE CORRESPONDENT: Humans landed on the moon for the first time over 50 years ago. Back then it was really only the U.S. and Russia that were in the space race. Now, the European Space Agency says there are over 100 lunar missions planned for the moon before 2030, and they're made up of countries and companies all over the world.
>
> So why is everyone racing to the moon again? Well, the main reasons are resources, exploration and politics.
>
> The moon is home to many resources, like rare earth minerals and elements like the isotope helium-3 that could prove useful to mine. Helium-3 is hard to find on Earth, but it can be found all over the moon, and is theoretically vital toward nuclear fusion — a possibly game-changing clean energy source.
>
> Studies have theorized that water in ice form may be available in large quantities on the moon, and mining could provide water and oxygen — the critical elements needed to make rocket fuel and allow further exploration.
>
> The U.S., Russia and China all have plans to establish permanent bases on the moon, offering a jumping-off point to explore Mars and go even deeper into outer space.
>
> But how legal is all of this potential activity on the moon? It outlines that the moon should be the province of all mankind and should be used exclusively for peaceful purposes. So military bases, nuclear weapons and weapons testing are forbidden.
>
> The treaty also stresses cooperation, but critics say that it lacks any means of enforcement, which could prove diplomatically dangerous if it's exploited.
>
> Now, in recent years, the United States has engaged in a new round of space diplomacy called the Artemis Accords. So far, there are more than 30 signatory nations, but notably China and Russia have not participated.
>
> Many scientists are concerned that mining and development could affect experiments and scientific projects that are already in place. So although there is this renewed rush to get to the moon, the future of diplomacy on the moon is a critical question.

**译**：主播：美国完成这一壮举的同时，其他国家也想迈出各自的"一大步"。有请太空与防务记者 Kristin Fisher。

月球资源丰富，比如稀土矿物和氦-3 等同位素，都值得开采。氦-3 在地球上极难获取，但月球上遍布，理论上对核聚变至关重要——那可能是改变游戏规则的清洁能源。
研究推测，月球上的水以冰的形式可能存在巨量储备，开采后可提供水与氧气，即制造火箭燃料、支撑更远探索的关键要素。
美、俄、中都计划在月球建立永久基地，把它作为跳板去探索火星、走向更深的太空。
但这些潜在活动在法律上站得住脚吗？1967 年《联合国外层空间条约》是多数国家签署的月球法基础，但专家认为它需要更新。条约规定月球是全人类的领地，只能用于和平目的，因此军事基地、核武器与武器试验都被禁止。条约也强调合作，但批评者指出它缺乏任何强制执行手段，一旦被人钻空子，在外交上会很危险。
近几年美国推动了一轮新的太空外交——《阿尔忒弥斯协定》，目前有 30 多个签署国，但引人注目的是中俄没有参加。
不少科学家担心，采矿与开发会影响已经在运行的实验与科研项目。所以，尽管重返月球的热潮再起，月球外交的未来仍是个关键问题。

## 四、语音与连读训练点（本集最值得反复听的地方）

1. **a bit of a nail-biter** — "bit of a" 三词连成一串 /ˈbɪtəvə/，中间 t 几乎不发音
2. **first U.S.-made spacecraft** — "U.S.-made" 读 /ˌjuː es ˈmeɪd/，重音落在 made
3. **engineers had to overcome** — had to /ˈhædtə/，to 弱读成 /tə/

5. **the province of all mankind** — "province of all" 三个词一气呵成，注意 /prəˈvɪnsəvɔːl/
6. **it lacks any means of enforcement** — "means of" 连读，enforcement 重音在 -force-
7. **surged past a trillion dollars** — "past a" 连读 /ˈpɑːstə/，trillion 的 /tr/ 要卷
8. **I don't want to spend gas money** — "want to" 常读 /ˈwɑːnə/（wanna），这是真实口语的关键
9. **kind of new terminology** — "kind of" /ˈkaɪndə/，of 几乎消失
10. **what people are kind of drawn to** — 句尾 "drawn to" 的 to 弱读，整句降调
